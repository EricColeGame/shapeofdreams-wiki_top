## Red Flags: How to Spot Fake Shape of Dreams Code Sites

The phrase "Shape of Dreams codes" attracts scam pages the same way any popular game does. These sites follow a predictable playbook, and once you know the signs, they're easy to dismiss.

| Red flag | What it looks like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Generators" | A tool claiming to create unlimited codes | Close the tab — these cannot work |
| Login walls | Asks for Steam, Discord, or Google credentials | Never enter credentials on a code site |
| Survey gates | "Complete 3 offers to reveal your code" | This is affiliate fraud, not a reward |
| Fake urgency | Countdown timers and "only 50 left" banners | Ignore; official announcements don't rush you |
| No source link | Codes listed without a link to an official post | Cross-check official channels first |
| Copy-paste lists | Identical text across dozens of domains | Treat as recycled and unverified |

These pages monetize through ad impressions, affiliate offers, and stolen account data. They don't need the codes to be real — they only need you to click. If a site can't show you a screenshot of the developer's own announcement, it has nothing.
