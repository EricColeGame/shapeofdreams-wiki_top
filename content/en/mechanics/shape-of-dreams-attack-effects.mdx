## What Counts as an Attack Effect in Shape of Dreams

Attack effects are everything your hit keeps doing after the damage number fades. In practice they arrive from three layers that sit on top of each other:

- **The skill layer.** Every Memory has an innate behavior — a projectile that pierces, a slam that knocks enemies back, a beam that leaves a lingering burn. This is the effect you always get, regardless of what else you build.
- **The modifier layer.** Essences slotted into a Memory can add a brand-new effect, amplify an existing one, or convert one type into another. This is where most of a build's identity comes from.
- **The character layer.** Each Dreamer starts with traits that bias them toward certain effects, so two players using the same Essence can end up with very different outcomes.

Because effects come from multiple layers, they don't just add — they multiply. A burn that ticks on its own is fine. A burn that ticks while the target is also shredded and chilled is a kill.

Community reports consistently point to the same conclusion: raw damage scaling is the least interesting lever in the game. Effect coverage is what turns a good run into a broken one. ## The Core Attack Effect Categories

Most shape of dreams attack effects fall into six families. Knowing which family you're building around keeps you from picking Essences that fight each other.

| Category | In-play signature | Strong against | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Damage over time | Health drains in ticks after the hit lands | Large health pools, bosses | Short-lived trash dies before the effect pays off |
| Hard crowd control | Freeze, stun, or root locks the target in place | Dangerous elites with big windups | Many bosses resist or shorten duration |
| Soft crowd control | Chill or slow reduces movement and attack speed | Fast swarms, ranged packs | Rarely stops a lethal attack on its own |
| Chain and spread | Shock or arc damage jumps between nearby targets | Dense rooms, co-op swarms | Falls off hard against isolated targets |
| Defense shred | Armor break or weaken makes everything else hit harder | Tanky enemies, damage-window bosses | Wasted if you have no burst to follow up |
| Sustain and drain | Lifesteal or heal-on-hit returns health as you fight | Attrition rooms, long boss phases | Does nothing if you can't keep attacking |

A few practical notes on each:

- **Damage over time** rewards applying early and often. If your DoT has a short duration, attack speed or multi-hit skills keep it refreshed better than one giant hit.
- **Hard crowd control** is a panic button, not a damage plan. The value isn't the frozen enemy — it's the three free seconds you get to reposition.
- **Chain effects** scale with enemy count. In solo play they're situational; in a four-player co-op run they can carry an entire floor.
- **Shred** is the quiet MVP. Any build with burst damage becomes dramatically stronger the moment something is stripping defenses.
- **Sustain** effects are trap-adjacent. They feel amazing in easy rooms and do nothing in the fights that actually kill you.

## How Attack Effects Stack, Refresh, and Expire

This is where most builds quietly break. Two effects that look like they should combine often don't, and two that look redundant sometimes multiply. Understanding the interaction rules is the difference between a theorycrafted build and one that actually performs.

| Interaction | Typical behavior | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Same DoT applied twice | Refreshes duration instead of adding a second stack | Increase uptime, not application count |
| Two different DoTs | Both tick independently | Stack different damage types when you can |
| Slow plus freeze | Chill usually still applies, but freeze overrides movement | Don't over-invest in slow if you already freeze |
| Shred plus burst | Shred multiplies the burst window | Line up your biggest skill with shred application |
| Multiple on-hit procs | Each rolls separately, but heavy proc stacking dilutes stats | Pick two proc effects, not five |
| Effects on an already-controlled target | Diminishing returns kick in fast | Save hard CC for when control drops |

The general rule that community testing keeps arriving at: effects have an invisible budget. Every Essence slot spent on a new effect is a slot not spent making an existing effect hit harder. Four mediocre effects usually lose to two well-supported ones.

There's also the refresh problem. A damage-over-time effect lasting a few seconds is only valuable if you can reapply it before it lapses. If your rotation has gaps, the effect's real uptime is far lower than the tooltip suggests — and your damage graph will show it.

## Build Archetypes That Live or Die by Their Effects

Once you understand the categories, you can pick a lane. The shape of dreams attack effects that define each archetype below are the ones players report as most consistent across runs.

| Archetype | Anchor effect | Stats to prioritize | Playstyle | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ignite stacker | Damage over time | Attack speed, effect duration | Apply early, kite, let ticks finish | Weak in short fights |
| Frost controller | Chill into freeze | Effect chance, area coverage | Lock down rooms, control tempo | Low raw output |
| Storm spreader | Chain and shock | Area size, chain count | Thrives in crowded rooms and co-op | Poor against solo elites |
| Bruiser drain | Sustain plus shred | Max health, lifesteal | Trade hits, out-sustain everything | Falls off against burst damage |
| Hybrid applier | Two complementary effects | Balanced offense and utility | Flexible in any lobby | Needs better Essence luck |

Two things separate the archetypes that work from the ones that don't.

First, every successful effect build has a **payoff skill** — one big ability that converts all the setup into damage. Burning an enemy for a long time means nothing if nothing cashes it in.

Second, every build needs a **fallback**. If your anchor effect is chain damage and the boss is alone in the arena, you need something that still functions. That's usually a shred, a raw damage Memory, or a defensive cooldown.

## Co-op Roles, Common Mistakes, and Fixes

In co-op, effect duty should be split. Four players all stacking burn means three of them are wasting slots.

| Role | Job on the team | Effect focus | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applier | Keeps effects on the target at all times | DoT, shred | Lowest damage, highest impact |
| Controller | Handles freezes, stuns, and interrupts | Hard and soft CC | Calls out when control is about to drop |
| Burster | Cashes in the setup window | Raw damage, crit | Must be told when shred lands |
| Anchor | Holds aggro and stays alive | Sustain, defense | Keeps the applier free to maintain uptime |

Now the mistakes that quietly cost runs:

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stacking four different effects | Stats get diluted, nothing scales | Pick two effects and support them |
| Ignoring refresh timing | DoT uptime is far lower than you think | Add attack speed or a reapply skill |
| Treating CC as damage | Control doesn't kill, it buys time | Use the window, don't admire it |
| Building effects with no payoff | Setup without a finisher is just slow damage | Always carry one burst option |
| Copying a co-op build in solo | Chain and aura effects need targets | Rebuild for single-target fights |

If you take one thing from this guide, take this: shape of dreams attack effects are a resource, and like any resource they're strongest when concentrated. Pick a lane, support it, and give it a way to finish the fight.

## FAQ

**Do attack effects in Shape of Dreams stack when multiple players apply them?**

Community reports suggest that identical damage-over-time effects from different players generally refresh rather than create a second full instance, while different effect types can coexist. In practice, the strongest co-op teams assign one player to each effect family instead of everyone running the same one.

**Is damage over time better than burst damage?**

They answer different questions. DoT effects are excellent against high-health targets and long boss phases, while burst is better in short rooms and against enemies that punish slow kills. Most stable builds carry one of each.

**Can I change my shape of dreams attack effects in the middle of a run?**

Yes, in most cases. Because effects come from Memories and the Essences slotted into them, swapping a Memory or replacing an Essence mid-run can shift your entire damage profile. The catch is that your existing stat picks may no longer support the new effect.

**Which attack effect should a new player start with?**

A single damage-over-time effect plus a shred is the friendliest starting point. Both are easy to keep active, both scale well into later rooms, and neither requires precise timing to feel rewarding.
